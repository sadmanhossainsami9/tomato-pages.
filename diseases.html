Tomato leaf diseases

These are the leaf conditions the model learns to tell apart. Each one has a typical look, a cause, and ways to reduce it.

Early blight

Caused by the fungus Alternaria solani

What it looks like

Dark brown spots with ring patterns, often with a yellow area around them. It usually starts on the older, lower leaves.

When it spreads

Warm, humid weather with frequent leaf wetness.

How to reduce it

Rotate crops, remove infected leaves, mulch the soil, and water at the base instead of over the leaves.

Late blight

Caused by the water mold Phytophthora infestans

What it looks like

Large, water-soaked patches that turn brown or dark and can dry into papery areas. White fuzzy growth may appear underneath in damp conditions.

When it spreads

Cool, wet weather. It can destroy a plant within days.

How to reduce it

Plant resistant varieties, leave space between plants for airflow, and remove and dispose of infected plants quickly.

Leaf mold

Caused by the fungus Passalora fulva

What it looks like

Pale green or yellow patches on the upper side of the leaf, with olive-green to brown velvety mold on the underside.

When it spreads

High humidity and poor airflow, common in greenhouses and crowded plantings.

How to reduce it

Improve ventilation, space and prune plants, keep leaves dry, and choose resistant varieties.

Septoria leaf spot

Caused by the fungus Septoria lycopersici

What it looks like

Many small, round spots with dark edges and tan or gray centers. It usually begins on lower leaves and moves upward.

When it spreads

Wet weather and splashing water that carries spores to the leaves.

How to reduce it

Remove affected lower leaves, avoid overhead watering, mulch, and rotate crops.

Bacterial spot

Caused by Xanthomonas bacteria

What it looks like

Small, dark, water-soaked spots that turn brown or black, sometimes with a yellow border. Leaves can look ragged as spots join.

When it spreads

Warm, wet conditions. Bacteria spread through splashing water, tools, and infected seed.

How to reduce it

Use disease-free seed and transplants, avoid handling wet plants, and rotate crops.

Target spot

Caused by the fungus Corynespora cassiicola

What it looks like

Brown spots with light centers and ring patterns, similar to early blight. Spots can grow and join, and may also appear on stems and fruit.

When it spreads

Warm, humid weather with long periods of leaf wetness.

How to reduce it

Space and prune plants for airflow, remove infected leaves and plant debris, water at the base, and rotate crops.

Tomato yellow leaf curl virus

Caused by a virus spread by whiteflies

What it looks like

Leaves curl upward and turn yellow at the edges, stay small, and the whole plant looks stunted.

When it spreads

Whiteflies carry the virus from plant to plant, mostly in warm weather.

How to reduce it

There is no cure. Control whiteflies, use resistant varieties and insect nets, and remove infected plants early.

Tomato mosaic virus

Caused by Tomato mosaic virus

What it looks like

A mottled pattern of light and dark green patches, with leaves that may look wrinkled, narrow or twisted.

When it spreads

It spreads by touch, through hands, tools and infected seed, and can survive on surfaces for a long time.

How to reduce it

Use clean seed, wash hands and disinfect tools, remove infected plants, and choose resistant varieties.

Spider mites

Caused by tiny pests, not a disease (for example Tetranychus urticae)

What it looks like

Many tiny yellow or pale speckles on the leaf, which can turn bronze and dry out. Fine webbing may appear on the underside.

When it spreads

Hot, dry weather, when mite numbers grow quickly.

How to reduce it

Rinse leaves with water, keep plants well watered, use insecticidal soap, and protect natural predators of mites.

Healthy leaf

No disease present

What it looks like

Even green color, a smooth surface, and no spots, mold, or curling.

Why it matters

The model needs healthy leaves as a class so it can tell a normal leaf from a diseased one.

How to keep it that way

Water at the base, give plants room to breathe, and check leaves regularly for early signs.
