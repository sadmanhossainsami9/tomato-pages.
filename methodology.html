Methodology

This page explains how the project goes from leaf photos to a disease prediction, and how a scan works on this website.

Project workflow

Six stages, from raw images to a working website.

1

Collect images

Photos of tomato leaves are gathered and labeled by condition: each disease class and healthy leaves.

2

Prepare the data

Images are resized and normalized to the format the model expects, and split into training and testing sets.

3

Train the model

A convolutional neural network, ResNet-50, learns the visual patterns that separate one condition from another.

4

Evaluate

The model is tested on images it has not seen during training to measure how often it predicts correctly.

5

Deploy as a service

The trained model runs on a server that accepts an image and returns its predictions.

6

Use it on the website

The Disease Detection page sends the visitor’s photo to that server and shows the result.

How a scan works here

What happens when a visitor taps Scan leaf.

  1. Upload. The visitor chooses or takes a photo of a leaf.
  2. Send. The page sends the photo to the model server.
  3. Predict. The model scores each disease class and returns the three most likely ones.
  4. Show. The page displays the top result with a confidence percentage, plus the next two possibilities.
  5. Check confidence. If the top score is below the confidence limit, the page says it is not sure and asks for a clearer photo.

Limitations

Results are predictions, not diagnoses. Models trained on photos with plain backgrounds can be less accurate on photos taken in a field, with uneven light or many leaves in the frame. Use a close, sharp photo of a single leaf, and check with a local agriculture expert before acting on a result.
